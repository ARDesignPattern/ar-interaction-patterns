Designing Reusable Interaction for Museum XR

A doctoral research project on interaction design for head-mounted augmented reality in museums — from first-time-user challenges and field prototypes to a parameterized and empirically evaluated interaction pattern language.

Pattern System

A Two-Level Interaction Pattern Language

The research resulted in a reusable interaction design system that connects broader interaction purposes with concrete, parameterized solutions for HMD-based museum experiences.

THREE

Category-Level Patterns

Higher-level interaction structures describing recurring roles within the visitor journey.

NINE

Application-Level Patterns

Concrete interaction solutions that can be instantiated, adapted, and combined across museum scenarios.

Parameterized

Adaptable Design Knowledge

Explicit parameters describe which aspects of a pattern can change across exhibits, environments, and visitor situations.

Composable

Reusable Interaction Flows

Multiple patterns can be combined to construct larger visitor journeys rather than functioning as isolated UI elements.

Pattern Categories

Three Recurring Roles in the Museum Experience

Category-level patterns organize the interaction system around recurring visitor needs: reaching relevant content, discovering available AR experiences, and engaging with exhibit-related information.

Empirical Foundation

Evidence Behind the Pattern System

The interaction patterns were developed and refined through complementary forms of evidence rather than through a single study.

Visitor Studies

HMD-based AR prototypes were evaluated with museum visitors and first-time users under realistic exhibition conditions.

Creator Evaluation

AR developers and cultural heritage practitioners examined the clarity, usability, and reuse potential of the pattern language.

Cross-Site Deployment

Related pattern compositions were adapted and deployed across different natural history museum environments.

Design-Parameter Studies

Specific dimensions, including semantic anchoring and spatial UI placement, were investigated through focused empirical studies.

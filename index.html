A Research Portal for HMD-Based AR Interaction Design Patterns

This website presents the PhD research of Yu Liu at Hochschule RheinMain, focusing on reusable, parameterized, and empirically validated interaction design patterns for Head-Mounted Display (HMD)-based Augmented Reality (AR) in museum and cultural heritage contexts.

Explore the Pattern System View Research Overview

Design Pattern System

This pattern system is structured around two main components:

  • Design Patterns: A two-level pattern system consisting of category-level classes and application-level patterns for HMD-based AR interaction design, presented through scenario descriptions, interaction flows, design parameters, and implementation-oriented descriptions.
  • Technology Module (under development): A library of reusable Unity-based prefabs that implement selected application-level patterns, to be published on GitHub for direct integration and further adaptation.

Research Context

This work is embedded in two European projects: presentXR and LoGa Culture, both exploring innovative AR interaction paradigms for museums and cultural institutions.

By bridging design theory and technical implementation, this project advances pattern-based methodologies in immersive media and invites collaboration and feedback from the global AR community.

Research Motivation

Why HMD-Based AR Needs Reusable Interaction Patterns

Design Challenge

Museums and cultural heritage institutions increasingly experiment with HMD-based AR to enrich visitor experiences. However, many AR applications remain isolated prototypes that are difficult to adapt, reuse, or transfer to new exhibits and museum settings.

At the same time, first-time visitors often have limited tolerance for training, unfamiliar interaction techniques, and attention-demanding interfaces. This creates a need for interaction solutions that are not only engaging, but also understandable, reusable, and adaptable to situated exhibition contexts.

Research Response

This research develops a two-level interaction pattern system for HMD-based AR in museums. The system organizes recurring interaction problems and solutions into category-level classes and application-level patterns, supported by design parameters, composition logic, and implementation-oriented descriptions.

The patterns are grounded in iterative prototype development, museum-based studies, expert feedback, and cross-site reconfiguration across cultural heritage scenarios.

Research Contribution

What This Portal Provides

Two-Level Pattern System

A structured collection of category-level classes and application-level patterns for organizing HMD-based AR interaction design in museum visits.

Pattern System

From Visitor Journey to Reusable Interaction Patterns

Category-Level Classes

Category-level classes describe broader functional roles within the museum visit, such as guiding visitors to points of interest, indicating available AR experiences, and presenting exhibit-related information.

They help organize the pattern language around recurring phases of HMD-based museum interaction.

Application-Level Patterns

Application-level patterns describe concrete and reusable interaction solutions, such as avatar-based guidance, spatial triggers, sequential explanation, AR labelling, reassembly tasks, feature drawing, and embodied object-catching interactions.

These patterns can be combined into interaction flows and adapted through configurable parameters according to exhibit content, spatial layout, visitor needs, and technical constraints.

Research Process

How the Pattern System Was Developed

1

Early Interaction Analysis

The research began with the analysis of interaction challenges when transferring AR interaction concepts from handheld devices to head-mounted displays in exhibition contexts.

2

Prototype-Led Exploration

Multiple HMD-based AR prototypes were developed and tested in relation to guidance, onboarding, exhibit interpretation, playful interaction, and visitor engagement.

3

Pattern Derivation

Recurring interaction problems and reusable solutions were abstracted from prototype iterations into a structured pattern schema with design parameters and composition relationships.

4

Evaluation and Reconfiguration

The resulting pattern system was examined through visitor-side deployments, creator-side expert evaluation, and cross-site adaptation across museum contexts.

Validation

Evidence Behind the Pattern System

Visitor-Side Evaluation

Museum visitors tested HMD-based AR prototypes under situated exhibition conditions, supporting the analysis of usability, workload, presence, attention, and interaction flow.

Creator-Side Evaluation

AR developers and cultural heritage practitioners assessed whether the patterns support planning, communication, implementation thinking, and exhibit-oriented adaptation.

Cross-Site Reconfiguration

Pattern compositions were adapted across comparable museum scenarios to examine which interaction roles, relationships, and parameters remain stable and which require local adjustment.

Technology Module

Toward Reusable Unity-Based AR Components

Reusable Prefabs

The technology module is under development as a library of Unity-based prefabs corresponding to selected application-level patterns. These prefabs are intended to support faster prototyping, adaptation, and implementation of HMD-based AR interactions.

GitHub Publication

The reusable resources will be published through GitHub to support research transparency, technical reuse, and further development by AR designers, developers, and cultural heritage researchers.

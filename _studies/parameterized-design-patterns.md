This study investigates how recurring interaction solutions from HMD-based museum prototypes can be transformed into reusable and adaptable design knowledge.

The research moved from comparing concrete interaction flows across different prototypes toward a parameterized, two-level pattern system that supports reuse, adaptation, and recombination across museum XR contexts.

## 1. Abstracting common interaction structures from multiple prototypes

The first stage of the research compared different HMD-AR prototypes at the level of their interaction flows.

Rather than treating each prototype as an isolated design case, the analysis examined where different experiences shared similar interaction purposes, sequences, and structural elements. These recurring similarities provided a basis for identifying higher-level interaction patterns that could describe broader design intentions beyond a single implementation.

This abstraction process supported the creation of higher-level patterns that could be used not only to document existing interaction solutions, but also to guide the recreation and adaptation of new museum XR experiences.

A central part of this work was identifying which aspects of a pattern should remain stable and which aspects should be adjustable. The research therefore introduced a method for identifying key parameters within recurring interaction structures, allowing patterns to be adapted to different exhibits, environments, and interaction situations.

The resulting parameterized patterns provided an intermediate step between concrete prototype behavior and a more general reusable design system.

## 2. Organizing the patterns into a composable two-level system

The parameterized patterns were subsequently organized into a two-level pattern system that distinguishes between broader category-level patterns and more concrete application-level patterns.

Category-level patterns describe recurring interaction purposes and higher-level structures, while application-level patterns represent more specific interaction solutions that can be instantiated within an HMD-AR museum experience.

The two levels are designed to work together. Higher-level patterns provide a structure for understanding and adapting interaction goals, while application-level patterns can be selected, parameterized, and combined to realize concrete interaction flows.

This organization supports composability: multiple patterns can be combined to form larger visitor experiences rather than being used as isolated interface elements. It also supports reuse by allowing designers to adapt the same interaction logic to different exhibits, content types, and spatial situations.

The resulting pattern language was evaluated through prototype instantiation, visitor studies, and creator/expert feedback. These evaluations provided evidence for refining the patterns, their parameters, and the overall organization of the system.

Rather than defining a fixed set of correct solutions, the system provides structured design knowledge that can be adapted and recombined according to the needs of different museum XR experiences.
